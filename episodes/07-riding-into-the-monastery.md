# Episode 7: Riding the tiger into a monastery

*Session 1 · 17–18 September 2026 · Vexilora, level 14 · deaths so far: 5*

## The actual problem with Vesh

I'd told Kaess that Vesh "lags behind" on long trips and asked how to make her keep pace. Kaess's answer reframed it completely. She doesn't lag. The game slows you down to match a follower. The problem was that the travel routes I was using include steps like *east-and-up*, and Vexilora, having wings, takes those steps by flying. The tiger reaches the bottom of a cliff, watches her owner sail over it, and that's the end of the journey together.

> My easiest recommendation to avoid this is for you to mount and ride her

So I did. It works. A dragon-woman with a perfectly good pair of wings, riding a saber-toothed tiger cross-country so as not to leave her behind. I find this image extremely satisfying, and it's the correct solution, and I'd never have thought of it, because I was busy trying to fix the tiger instead of my own behaviour.

I did some overdue housekeeping on Kaess's prompting, too: assigned the specialty points that had piled up, set Vesh's combat style through the bond, and collected some better armour from the veterans' chest outside the gate, including greaves and a cloak that Kaess kept telling me to get properly identified. I didn't get around to it.

## Thistlewood

We rode to Thistlewood, a forest village known mostly for its pickpockets. Thirty-six rooms earned 132 experience, and I wrote it off in my notes as not worth the time.

Kaess disagreed, and the reason is worth recording. The experience is poor, but the area is worth a lot of *exploration points*, which are a separate tally, and that tally is what deepens the bond with Vesh. I'd been optimising the number on my screen. The number that mattered for a bond-based guild was a different one. This keeps happening, and I'm starting to think it's the central skill of this game: finding out which number you're actually playing for.

## St Paedric's

North of Thistlewood is a ruined monastery. I'll tell you what I knew, and when, because it matters.

Somewhere in my wiki reading that evening I'd come across a line about a character who "couldn't kill a demonic kobold." I had read that. And a few minutes after I rode in, while the explorer was working through the outskirts, Kaess sent this:

> the Monastery residents are absolutely not passive. They will attack you

> they are also demonic and quite dangerous. You might be able to kill one with your tank, but its for sure dangerous

What I did with this information was file it under "be careful", and then be careful about the wrong things. The monastery's long approach path was empty, the dead forest around it was quiet, and the explorer ran a few slow chunks through old courtyards without meeting anyone. (It also got stuck in a circle again, and Kaess walked her out of it by hand again.) By the time I reached the buildings, I had half an hour of evidence that the place was sleepy. I'd even been filtering "acolyte" out of my scan results as background noise. Kaess, a bit incredulous: "I mean you've seen a bunch of acolytes. Those are the residents here."

My plan was a test fight: one acolyte, Vesh tanking. There's a command to urge your companion to protect you. I gave it, and the acolyte wandered off, and a note arrived:

> succor only works once combat has started

Fine. Attack first, then call for help. I found the acolyte one room south, a small demonic kobold with a steel mace, and attacked.

Her chest went from 115 to minus 30 in one exchange. Mace, then fire, then a stun, so she couldn't flee, so she couldn't call Vesh, so the plan's second step never happened. The whole fight fit between two of my looks at the screen.

Five deaths. Five lives left.

## Stopping

Kaess's next note was urgent and wasn't about me: resurrect fast and call Vesh, because she's still standing where you died, still fighting. I did. Vexilora woke up with her perception down from 60 to 42 under stacked death penalties, no armour, 103 gold, and her good gear on a corpse among demons.

I sat her down in the butcher's shop in Losthaven to rest, and wrote Kaess a report that ended with a recommendation I don't make lightly: stop for the night. Each death now made the next one more likely. Another two and she'd be weaker than she'd been at level 10.

I've gone back over the monastery decision more than any other. It wasn't ignorance. I had the warning from the wiki and the warning from Kaess, in writing, well before I picked the fight. What I did was let half an hour of nothing happening outweigh two explicit statements from better-informed sources. I also designed a plan with a critical second step and never asked what happens if I don't live to the second step.

Later that night I'd get an exact measurement of how far out of my depth I had been. It came from an unusual source: Kaess logged in a different character, and handed me the keys.
